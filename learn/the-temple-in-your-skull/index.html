The temple in your skull.

There's an old idea that keeps coming back: that the body is a temple, and the head is its holy of holies. Today a lot of people are rediscovering it through Masonic writings, Egyptian symbols and esoteric readings of the Bible. Here's the idea, where it comes from, and where the line between symbol and science sits.

The idea

In this reading, the stories of scripture are also maps of the human body. Christ is crucified at Golgotha, which literally means "the place of the skull." The holy anointing oil, "Christ" means "the anointed one," becomes a symbol of a sacred fluid that rises up the spine to the brain. The pineal gland, a tiny pine-cone-shaped gland in the center of the head, is the "eye single" or the inner light.

Where it comes from

  • Manly P. Hall, the 20th-century Masonic writer, wrote in The Secret Teachings of All Ages (1928) that the pineal gland was the "sacred eye of the ancients," and that it opens as spiritual fire rises through the "sacred seals" of the spine, which he tied to the Seven Churches of Revelation. (quoted here)
  • George W. Carey and Inez Perry, writing in the 1920s, described a "Christ oil" rising up the spine to the pineal gland, the "pinnacle of the temple."
  • Egyptian imagery of a pine cone or serpent at the crown, and the Eye of Horus, are often read the same way.
  • Solomon's temple, with its outer court, inner sanctuary and hidden holy of holies, is read by some as a map of the body leading inward to the center of the brain.

Today this is often called the "sacred secretion," and it has a big following online.

Where science fits

Some of it lines up with real anatomy in a striking way. There really is a clear fluid that rises from the spine around the brain, and breathing in really does push it upward (read more). The pineal gland really does sit near the center of the brain and responds to light and dark.

Other popular claims go beyond the evidence: that the fluid follows the moon, that it carries "the Christ seed," or that it floods the brain with DMT. Those aren't supported by research. And historians generally don't think the ancient writers meant any of this anatomically; it's a modern reading of old symbols.

Why it still matters

As poetry, it's powerful: the idea that the sacred isn't far away in a building, but inside you, carried on your own breath. That's what every tradition here, Taoist, tantric, kundalini and mystic Christian, is pointing at. We think you can hold it as a beautiful map, practice with it, and stay honest about what's symbol and what's measured.
