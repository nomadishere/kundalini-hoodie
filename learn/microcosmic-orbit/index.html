The Microcosmic Orbit, explained.

Up the back, over the head, down the front, and around again. That's the whole map. Taoist masters called it the small heavenly circuit. In the West it's known as the Microcosmic Orbit, and it's the same loop the glow hood's lights trace.

Two rivers in one body

Chinese medicine describes two main channels running along the body's center line. The Governing Vessel (Du Mai) runs from the perineum up the spine, over the crown and down to the upper lip. The Conception Vessel (Ren Mai) runs from the lower lip down the front of the body back to the perineum. One is called the sea of yang, the other the sea of yin. (overview)

Put them together and you get a closed loop. The practice is learning to feel energy move around it on purpose.

The tongue is the switch

The two channels meet at the mouth, but they don't touch on their own. That's why teachers ask you to rest the tip of your tongue on the roof of your mouth, just behind the teeth. It closes the circuit, like flipping a switch. The same instruction shows up in kundalini, tantra and Taoist practice: tongue up, jaw soft.

Where it comes from

Taoists trace the orbit back to the inner alchemy schools of medieval China, including teachings credited to Lü Dongbin, one of the Eight Immortals. Mantak Chia brought it to the West with his 1983 book Awaken Healing Energy Through the Tao, which is still the most popular way people learn it. (Qi Encyclopedia)

How to practice it

  1. Sit tall, tongue on the roof of your mouth, breathing through your nose.
  2. Rest your attention a few inches below your navel. Feel warmth gather there.
  3. On a slow inhale, move that warmth down to the perineum and up the spine: low back, between the shoulder blades, back of the neck, crown.
  4. On the exhale, let it pour down the front: brow, throat, heart, solar plexus, belly.
  5. Do 9 to 36 slow rounds, then rest your attention back in the belly.

Teachers say go slowly. If you feel pressure in the head, bring your focus back down to the belly and breathe normally.

The glow hood connection

The glow hood lights the orbit for you: up the spine on the inhale, crown at the top, down the front on the exhale. The lights don't move the energy. They give your attention a path to follow, which is the hardest part for beginners.

Honest note

Science hasn't measured "qi" or these channels directly. What is measured: breathing in moves fluid up the spine toward the head (read more). The orbit may be one of the oldest descriptions of that feeling.
